## Honest reading: this doesn't replicate BankNifty's result

**On Crude, the gate makes things worse, not better.** Trade count fell by more than half (28→12/mo), but win
rate barely moved (28.0%→28.6%) and PF actually *dropped* (1.09→1.02). Net points collapsed from +2,136 to
+249 -- an 88% reduction. The gate is filtering out roughly half the trades essentially at random with respect
to quality, not selectively removing the bad ones.

**On Gold and Silver, the gate gives a small, real PF improvement (1.07→1.11, 1.06→1.13) but at the cost of
roughly half the trade count, and net points are flat-to-down** (Gold −15%, Silver ~unchanged). This is not
the pattern the brief hypothesized ("cuts weak signals, raises win rate materially, increases average winner
size") -- win rate barely moves on any of the three (28.6%, 29.8%, 27.3% vs 28.0%, 29.7%, 28.7% baseline).

**Why this differs from BankNifty v0.4:** v0.4's ADX gate works because its underlying signal (EMA pullback +
wick + volume + VWAP reclaim) has a real quality gradient that ADX happens to separate -- weak-trend reclaims
genuinely are lower-quality setups for that specific entry mechanic. SHA-flip's entry condition (a Heikin-Ashi
colour change) doesn't have the same relationship to 15m ADX -- a flip can mark a genuine new trend at low ADX
readings (which the gate then discards) just as often as it does at high ADX. The gate is a good idea in
general, but it doesn't transfer between structurally different entry mechanics just because both are
intraday trend strategies.

**None of the three clears the requested PF>=1.3, and none is a net improvement over the untuned baseline in
total points.** This is a real, useful negative result: it rules out "just add the ADX gate" as the fix, and
says the option-buying cost problem (5-6x higher costs than futures per the brief's own model) is not solved
by this filter -- if anything, the sharply reduced trade count on Crude, combined with basically unchanged
per-trade quality, makes the option-cost coverage problem *worse* there, not better.
