Anatomy of bo(3) vs Silver #1 (30 months, gross)

What bo(3) actually is. It is described as “Silver #1 plus a breakout entry”, but 757 of its 836 trades (90.5%) are triggered by the breakout; only 39 are pure SHA flips and 40 coincide with a flip. Run with no SHA flip at all, a 3-bar breakout gives +329,025 gross vs +337,941 with it, and identical drawdown. So it is really a 3-bar breakout strategy with EMA9/22 trend filter that borrows Silver #1's stops and risk rules; the SHA flip contributes ~2.6%.

What is and is not established

Established
  • bo(3) leads Silver #1 on every basis measured except 2024 and no-cost drawdown.
  • Harness and TradingView agree (0.03% on drawdown over a 72-trade overlap).
  • All three lookbacks (3/5/10) beat #1 on the harness — a plateau, not one lucky setting.
  • Both #1 and the breakouts have a total distinguishable from zero.
Not established
  • That bo(3) beats #1 out of sample: paired monthly sign test p = 0.150; 95% CI on the difference spans zero.
  • Both strategies were tuned on the same 30 months; one silver move (Jan–Mar 2026) dominates all of them.
  • Drawdown: 71,324 gross vs 33,693 for #1, but 43,748 vs 38,708 after costs — treat as a range.
  • bo(5) loses to #1 on TradingView’s replay; only bo(3) holds up there.

Crude #1 — session-start tuning and 248-trial search — updated 27 Sep 2026

One basis for everything here: MCX:CRUDEOIL1! 5m, harness on TradingView-harvested bars, 30 months (25 Mar 2024 – 24 Sep 2026) unless marked TV replay (13 tiled ~81-day windows, 18 Jan 2024 – 25 Sep 2026, gross only). “Crude #1” is working_strategies/CrudeOil/1_v4.0_SHA_flip_RR4.0_recal.pine.txt: SHA(10/10) flip + EMA9/22 alignment, 10-bar swing stop floored at 1.5×ATR, skipped if stop > 3.0×ATR, fixed 4R target, one position, holds overnight, 09:15–23:30 session. Points, no costs unless marked after-cost (0.02%/side, ~2.6 pts/trade).

Session-start variants (same script, only the entry-window start changes)

This is the only change across the whole crude search that is confirmed real: it held up on the harness and on TradingView replay, in both halves of the data. Total points barely move — a later start just removes the low-value morning/afternoon trades, so profit per trade and PF rise while frequency falls.

326 tuning trials, 7 pre-registered rounds — one confirmed win

Crude v4.2: reversal exit, no fixed target — TradingView-confirmed, 27 Sep

Round 7's one real lead: same entry and stop as crude #1 (SHA flip + EMA9/22, 10-bar swing stop, 1.5–3.0×ATR), but no fixed 4R target — the position closes only on its stop, or on a reversal exit (an opposite valid SHA-flip+trend signal), filled at the next bar's open. Tested on the harness (30mo) and confirmed independently on TradingView tiled replay (13 windows). Win rate drops to ~19–21% (fewer, much bigger wins) and average hold time rises to multiple days — a real behavioural change, not just a number.

Established
  • Crude v4.2 (reversal exit, no target) beats crude #1 on every axis at 09:15, confirmed on both the harness and TradingView replay — the first genuine improvement found in 326 trials.
  • Later entry starts (15:00–18:00) raise PF, points/trade and after-cost profit at every start tested for crude #1 itself, on both the harness and TradingView replay.
  • Crude #1's edge is almost entirely long trades: short-only is roughly breakeven at every start.
  • The 3×ATR stop-width cap and SHA(10/10) lengths are load-bearing — every attempt to loosen either one lost money. 25 non-SHA entry triggers (round 6) all lost too — the SHA flip's selectivity is the edge, not an accident.
Not established
  • Crude v4.2 at the 17:30 start — harness said it should pass; TradingView shows it doesn't.
  • Any exit change, EMA re-tune, or entry filter tried in rounds 1–5 as a real edge — every apparent winner's gain sat entirely in the second half of the data.
  • The EMA9-slope filter (round 4's only pass in that phase) failed its own plateau check one round later (round 5).
  • No forward test is running for crude v4.2 yet — TradingView-replay-confirmed is not the same as live-confirmed.
