# Crude #1: round 6, entry signals OTHER than SHA flip (2026-09-27), 50 configs. Cumulative trials 298.
Pre-registration: `pre_registration_crude_round6_newsignals_2026_09_27.md`. Code `crude_round6_newsignals.py`, data `research_data/crude_round6.csv`.
25 signal families (EMA cross, MACD, Supertrend, Donchian breakout, opening-range breakout, RSI cross/reversion, Bollinger breakout/squeeze, Stochastic, CCI, VWAP cross, 3-bar momentum,
Keltner breakout, prior-day pivot breakout, DI+/DI- cross, Parabolic SAR flip, EMA5/13 cross), each x 2 entry starts, all using crude #1's own risk framework (10-bar swing stop floored at
1.5xATR, skipped if stop > 3.0xATR, fixed 4R target, one position) so the comparison isolates the trigger alone.
Controls: crude #1 (SHA flip) at 09:15: 785 tr, PF 1.263, +5,614 (TRAIN +2,270 / HOLDOUT +3,344). At 17:30: 429 tr, PF 1.476, +6,558 (+2,447 / +4,111).

**Result: every one of the 50 configurations is worse than crude #1, most by a wide margin. None passes the pre-registered rule (none was even eligible to try).**
- **PF ceiling across all 50 is 1.320** (Supertrend @17:30, only 190 trades) &mdash; every alternative trigger tops out below crude #1's PF of 1.263-1.476, usually well below.
- **Most are flat or lose money**: EMA9/22 cross +3,108 (09:15) / -367 (17:30); EMA9/22+EMA200 -1,548 / -731; RSI-cross-50 -3,408 / -555; Stochastic -917 / -189; PSAR flip -2,014 / +1,216;
  CCI zero-cross +1,470 / +4,509 (the best of the oscillators, still under half of crude #1's after-cost profit); opening-range breakout is unusable (33-42 trades total over 30 months, PF 0.23-0.72).
- **Trade counts are 1.5-5x crude #1's** (up to 3,543 for Stochastic) because these triggers fire far more often than a SHA flip, and the extra trades are net-negative &mdash; volume without an edge.
- **Selection transfer is negative**: Spearman(TRAIN, HOLDOUT) across the 50 = -0.28, i.e. the family that looked best on the first half (ema5x13, TRAIN +3,934) was among the worst on the second half (HOLDOUT -3,781, after-cost -5,848).
- **Best individually on gross full-period PF, for reference (none is a candidate):** Supertrend flip (PF 1.320 @17:30 / 1.252 @09:15, but only 190-404 trades and negative TRAIN); Donchian(20) breakout (PF 1.244 @17:30, 532 trades, close to but below crude #1); DI+/DI- cross (PF 1.226 @17:30, +6,287 gross, but TRAIN only +1,701 vs control's +2,447 and it needed 960 trades to get there &mdash; 2.2x crude #1's trade count for less profit).
- **Why crude #1 (SHA flip) wins**: the smoothed Heikin-Ashi flip fires only ~1,600 times in 30 months and, combined with the ATR stop-width cap, keeps just 785 of them &mdash; a high-selectivity filter. Every other trigger tested fires 2-7x more often on noise, and the fixed 4R target / swing stop pays for that noise in commissions and small losses. This is consistent with the round 1-5 finding that loosening any part of crude #1 (session, stop cap, EMA lengths) that increases trade count hurts profit.

**Verdict: the SHA-flip trigger is not an accident of crude #1's design; it is the reason it works.** Switching to a different, more common technical trigger (moving-average cross, oscillator cross, channel breakout, volatility breakout) with the same risk management loses money in every one of 50 tests. This closes the "different trigger" line of inquiry for crude unless a slower/rarer variant of one of these (e.g. Donchian on a longer lookback, or Supertrend with a higher multiplier) is worth one more narrow round &mdash; not attempted here since none came close enough to justify it.
