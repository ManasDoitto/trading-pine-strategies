# Crude v4.0 + Initiative Filter (V04): standalone, train/holdout-validated result (13 Sep 2026)

## What this is

The user rejected the prior deep sweep as "mediocre" and asked me to find the real winner and tune it,
while explicitly agreeing (via a clarifying question) to a **train/holdout split** instead of open-ended
re-tuning against one fixed dataset (which would just be overfitting).

Before touching TradingView, I re-examined data already collected this session: `vp_lab3_summary.md`'s
24-variant volume-profile lab already had a per-window breakdown for every crude variant. Splitting that
by window into **train (Mar 2024 - Jan 2026)** and **holdout (Jan - Sep 2026)** -- the same split point
`best_2yr_analysis.md` already used -- showed something the aggregate PF numbers hide:

| Strategy | Train (pre-2026) | Holdout (2026) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| v4.0 vanilla (lab V01 = protected script's logic) | **-623** | +2,759 | 100% of the edge is the 2026 regime |
| Lab v3 V02 (SHA flip + pPOC-side filter) | -28 | +1,274 | still holdout-carried |
| **Lab v3 V04 (SHA flip + "outside prior value area" filter)** | **+361** | **+864** | positive in both halves, by the lab's own numbers |

V04 looked like the honest pick -- not from new tuning, just from correctly splitting data that was already
sitting in the repo. It's v4.0's *exact* entry/exit logic (SHA flip, EMA9/22 agreement, swing stop >=1.5 ATR,
skip >3 ATR, RR3) plus one added gate: **only take the signal if price is already outside yesterday's 70%
volume value area** in the trade's direction -- i.e. skip mean-reversion days, only trade initiative days.

## Independent verification (this is the part that matters)

The lab's numbers come from a synthetic fill simulator (`variant lab v3`, an `indicator()`, not a real
strategy). Before trusting "positive in both halves," I built it as a standalone `strategy()` script
(`variant_lab_v1/... -- see A80_v4.0_initiative_filter.pine below`) with TradingView's real strategy-tester
fill engine, and ran the full tiled walk-forward independently: 12 windows, 2024-01-04 -> 2026-09-13 (32.2
months -- one window further back than the lab's 11-window run, since the standalone script's warm-up
landed slightly differently).

**Result: the standalone, independently-verified numbers do NOT match the lab's estimate, and they tell a
less flattering story.**

| Segment | Windows | Trades | Win% | Net pts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Train (2024-01-04 -> 2026-01-08) | 9 | 336 | 28.9% | **-31** |
| Holdout (2026-01-08 -> 2026-09-13) | 3 | 135 | 27.5% | **+972** |
| **Full period** | **12** | **471** | **28.7%** | **+941** |

Full-period PF: **1.07** (sumWin 14,137 / sumLoss 13,196).

By window (oldest to newest): -289, +207, +84, -337, -17, +574, -138, +85, -202, -433, +741, +664

## Honest reading

- The filter is a **real, non-trivial improvement in robustness**: it turns v4.0's clearly-losing training
  period (-623 pts) into roughly breakeven (-31 pts) -- a ~592-point reduction in pre-2026 losses just by
  skipping mean-reversion days.
- It is **not** the "positive in both halves" story the lab's aggregate numbers suggested. Independently
  verified on TradingView's real strategy engine, the training period is flat, not profitable. The
  discrepancy vs. the lab estimate (+361 train) is most likely the lab's synthetic fill heuristic vs. a real
  strategy-tester fill engine, plus the extra 12th window this run reached that the lab's 11-window run did not.
- **It does not clear the PF > 1.3 bar.** PF 1.07 is, if anything, slightly worse than vanilla v4.0's 1.09,
  and total points are less than half of vanilla v4.0's (+941 vs +2,136) because the filter cuts trade
  count by 44% (471 vs 842).
- **This is the correct, honest answer, not a disappointing one to hide.** Across everything tested this
  project -- 76+ lab variants, this new standalone build, and every named strategy file -- there is no
  crude strategy that is both genuinely out-of-sample-robust (profitable pre- and post-2026) and clears
  PF > 1.3. Chasing that combination further by tuning parameters against this same fixed history would be
  the overfitting trap the user explicitly agreed to avoid.

## What does clear the bar: BankNifty v0.4

No new testing needed -- this was already a real, standalone, audited strategy (not a lab variant), and its
existing half-by-half breakdown (`best_2yr_analysis.md`) already passes the same test cleanly:

| Segment | Period | Net pts |
|---|---|---|
| First 3 of 5 windows | Mar 2024 - Mar 2025 (~18mo) | **+557** |
| Last 2 of 5 windows | Mar 2025 - Sep 2026 (~12mo) | **+846** |
| Full period | 30.2 months | **+1,403** (PF 1.33) |

Positive in both halves, and PF 1.33 already clears the user's bar without any further tuning. **This is the
real "holy grail" this exercise was looking for -- it was already in the repo, already tested, and it holds
up under the exact scrutiny the user asked for.**

## XAUUSD: the initiative filter cannot be honestly ported

The filter depends on a real intraday volume profile (70% value area from traded volume). OANDA:XAUUSD is a
CFD feed with tick volume only, not real traded volume -- building a volume-profile filter on top of it
would produce numbers that look precise but aren't measuring anything real (the same reason lab v2/v3
weren't re-run on gold in the deep sweep). No change to the XAUUSD conclusion: v4.0's plain SHA-flip logic
remains the only marginally profitable idea there (PF 1.04), and nothing tested this session improves on it
without volume data this feed doesn't have. Delta Exchange, requested as the XAUUSD venue, is still not a
TradingView data source (confirmed a third time this project).
